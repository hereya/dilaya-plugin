# Put your app online (website)

Your software can go from "in the conversation" to a **real website** other people use from their browser, with no AI in the loop.

## What it involves

It takes several steps, which **your assistant does for you** — but a few decisions are yours:

1. **Who is allowed in?** By default your site is **private**: nobody enters without being invited. You give the **email addresses** of the people allowed.
2. **Sign-in without a password** — your users receive a **code by email** to sign in. Nothing to remember.
3. **Which pages are public?** Usually everything is protected. You can ask for some pages to be open to all.

## How to ask

> "Dilaya, put my app **online**. The people allowed are marie@example.com and paul@example.com. Everything must require a sign-in."

It all gets set up: sign-in by email, the access list, the site, and **the address** to share.

## Your own address (optional)

Your app already has a working address. You can also give it **your own**, something like `orders.mycompany.com`.

### The simplest way: ask Dilaya to buy it

Nothing to configure anywhere. You say the name you want, Dilaya checks whether it is free, tells you the price, buys it and attaches it to your app.

> "Dilaya, find me an address for my hair salon."

> "Dilaya, buy **myvenue-bookings.com** and put it on this app."

It renews itself every year, and you can stop that whenever you like from your space.

### Already own a domain elsewhere?

That works too: leave it where it is and add a few lines at your provider. You are told exactly which ones, one at a time.

## Prompts to copy

> "With Dilaya, publish a site where my customers check the status of their order, sign-in by email."

> "In Dilaya, add my domain **bookings.myvenue.com** to this app."
